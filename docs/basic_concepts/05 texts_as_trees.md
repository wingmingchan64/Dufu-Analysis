# Texts As Trees

## Representing Texts: Two Distinct Structures

Any meaningful text can be represented in two distinct ways:

- A flat (one-layer) structure of collection of linguistic units (e.g. words, phrases, or 字)
- A hierarchical layers of larger and smaller linguistic units

---

## Flat Structure

- The root, representing the entire text, has n children, where each child is a language-specific minimal linguistic/semantic units in the texts (e.g. words, phrases, or 字)
- All these children are terminal nodes
- These terminal nodes are ordered sequentially
- The depth of every leaf is 1

---

## Hierarchical structure

- Linguistic units in a text can be grouped hierarchically according to some syntactic/semantic considerations/criteria
- Relevant layers in such a tree: paragraphs, lines, segments (like phrases, clauses, or sentences), words, 字, etc.

# Texts as Trees

## Representing Texts: Two Forms of Tree Representation

A text can be represented as an ordered tree in two basic forms:

- Flat Structure: A single layer of ordered textual units beneath the root
- Hierarchical Structure: Multiple layers of textual units organized according to specified structural, syntactic, semantic, or editorial criteria

---

## Flat Structure

- The root represents the entire text
- The root has n children, each representing a basic textual unit, such as a character, word, or phrase, depending on the language and the chosen representation
- All children of the root are leaf nodes, arranged in a specified sequence
- Every leaf has a depth of 1

---

## Hierarchical structure

- Textual units can be grouped into larger units according to specified structural, syntactic, semantic, or editorial criteria
- Possible levels include paragraphs, lines, segments (such as phrases, clauses, or sentences), words, and characters
- The resulting structure is an ordered tree in which the hierarchical relationships and the sequence of textual units are preserved
